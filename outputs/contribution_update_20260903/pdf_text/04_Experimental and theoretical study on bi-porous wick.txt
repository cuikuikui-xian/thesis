ABSTRACT
The driving force of the fluid in passive phase-change devices is provided by the porous wick, and thus, the porous wick is one of the key fac-
tors determining the performance of these devices. In this work, the properties of sintered nickel powder porous wicks were systematically
studied to give a comprehensive evaluation. Three particle sizes (average diameter 2 –20 lm), pore-forming agent concentration (0% –30%wt),
and two preparation methods [loose sintering (LS) and cold press sintering] were utilized to prepare 42 samples. The results showed that
with the increase in particle size and pore-forming agent concentration and the decrease in preparation pressure, the capillary performance
was enhanced due to the improvement in the connectivity of internal pores. However, the strength of the wick decreased. Additionally, an
improved model for the capillary wick performance and permeability was proposed by combining the traditional Kozeny –Carman permeabil-
ity formula with an enhanced permeability equation based on the research outcomes of Byon and Kin. The improved model was specifically
optimized for scenarios involving the pore-forming agent. The predicted results agreed well with the experimental data, thoroughly validating
the effectiveness of the developed model.

I. INTRODUCTION
A. Background and research status
Passive phase-change heat dissipation methods utilizing capillary
force have garnered attention for cooling electronic devices due to their
lack of moving parts and external power requirements. Heat pipes
(HP), including traditional heat pipes, vapor chambers (VC), loop heat
pipes (LHP), and capillary pumped loops (CPL), are key passive
phase-change devices. HPs offer advantages, such as small temperature
differences during heat transfer, high heat-transfer capacity, long
transfer distances, and flexible configurations, making them ideal for
microgravity environments like spacecraft and satellites.
1,2 They are
also widely used in terrestrial applications, including central processing
units (CPUs)/graphics processing units (GPUs), light emitting diode
lights (LED lights),
3,4 nuclear reactors,5 and solar heat utilization.6
With increasing interest in space exploration since the 20th cen-
tury, numerous satellites have been launched, highlighting the need for
efficient heat dissipation as electronic device integration on these satel-
lites grows.7,8 The capillary force generated by porous wicks drives the
working fluid cycle in passive phase-change devices; thus, the perfor-
mance of these devices is significantly influenced by the wick ’s struc-
ture and properties.
9–12
According to the Laplace–Young Equation, the average radius of
the wick pores has an inverse proportionality with the capillary force.13
However, when the pore radius decreases, the flow resistance of fluid
inside the wick increases, which leads to a conflict between the increas-
ing capillary force and the decreasing flow resistance. Dependent on a
variety of factors, for example, materials, particle size, preparation
pressure, sintering temperature, porosity, and pore size of the porous
wick, these factors have a significant effect on the performance of pas-
sive phase-change devices. The porous wicks that are widely used are
groove,
14 sintered wire meshes, 15 metal foam, 16 and sintered metal
powder.17,18 For widely used sintered metal powder wicks with the
best comprehensive performance, the effective pore radius is mainly
determined by particle size, 19 whose impact on the heat pipe ’s heat
transfer characteristics has been investigated by some researchers.
Deng et al.
20 prepared various nickel powder wicks using four different
particle sizes (<50–150 lm) and found that as particle size increased,
permeability and performance improved. Kirdponpattaraet al.21 found
that the wick sintered by larger particles had a smaller contact angle
and that the performance was closely relevant to the particle size of the
wick, which was also of great importance in phase-change heat trans-
fer. Li et al.
18 reported that the particle size of copper powder wicks
had a considerable effect on the performance of LHP and experimen-
tally revealed that the optimum particle size fell between 75 and
100 lm. To improve the capillary performance of sintered powder
wicks, they also prepared nanostructures on sintered copper particles
by the alloying–dealloying method.
22 According to Weibel et al.,23 for
a wick with a certain thickness, the boiling heat transfer coefficient
peaked when the particle size was in the range of 106 –150 lm.
According to research by Xu et al.24 on the relationship between LHP
performance and particle size, startup and heat transfer performance
were at their best when the wick’s particle size was 139lm.
Except for particle size, the performance of a sintered powder wick
is also affected by particle distribution,25 packing process,26 and sintering
parameters.27 For example, Linet al.25 found that powder with uniform
particle size had the best capillary performance, which could lead to the
best thermal performance of heat pipes. Wuttijumnonget al.
27 reported
that the sintering performance had a remarkable influence on the
dimensions and strength of the porous wick. Nevertheless, the traditional
way of selecting material, particle size, preparation method, and sintering
temperature to control the internal porous structure of a wick has great
limitations in regulating pore radius and porosity. For instance, while the
loose sintering (LS) method is a better way to achieve high porosity, it
would weaken the wick’s strength. In an operating HP, a small portion
of the pores adhering to the heat substrate provides capillary force, while
t h er e s tj u s to f f e r sap a t hf o rt h el i q u i d’s flow. Hence, a wick with one
constant pore radius is insufficient for HP. Recently, to deal with the
shortcomings of the conventional wick, various researchers prepared bi-
porous wicks by mixing a soluble pore-forming agent into the metal
powder. Mathewset al.
28 sintered a multi-layer copper-nickel wick using
C10H8 as a pore-forming agent and investigated how the sintering tem-
perature affected the wick’s thermal conductivity, porosity, permeability,
and pore size. He et al.29 studied how the pore-forming agent’sp a r t i c l e
size and mass ratio affected the porosity, permeability, and capillary effi-
cacy. The results showed that the permeability and capillary performance
of the wick were influenced by the structure, size, and distribution of the
p o r e si na d d i t i o nt op o r o s i t y .W uet al.
30 prepared bi-porous wicks by
using polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) as a pore-forming agent and
claimed that the 250–297lm sized and 35 vol. % PMMA brought the
best thermal performance of a LHP. Li et al.,
31 Yeh et al.,32 and Li
et al.33 fabricated bi-porous wicks (particle radius¼ 2.2–3 lm) for LHP.
The impact of the pore-forming agent concentration on the wick ’s
porosity, permeability, and capillary performance was investigated.
To summarize, the sintered metal powder wick is more favorable
in phase change devices because of two reasons: (1) high thermal con-
ductivity leads to high boiling/evaporating efficiency; (2) easy to adhere
to the baseplate of the evaporator, which can reduce the contact thermal
resistance. On this basis, bi-porous wicks with small and large pores,
multi-layer wicks with different materials, and multi-scale wicks with
micro/nanostructures have been reported to be more effective.
22,28–33
B. Motivations of this study
Although many scholars have done research on sintered metal
powder wicks for heat pipes, there is still a lack of a general fabrication
process, which makes it difficult to fabricate the wicks with controllable
comprehensive performance in batches and to fulfill the cooling
requirements of the rapidly advancing electronics. The main reason is
that there are many factors that affect the sintered powder wick, such
as molding process, sintering temperature, particle size, particle shape,
pore former concentration. However, most of the literature only con-
siders one or two of these factors. For instance, Xin et al.
34 conducted
experimental investigations on capillary wick using nickel-copper
alloys, exploring the effects of different sintering methods, specifically
cold pressing and loose sintering, on the performance of the capillary

III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A. Microstructure of pores
Figure 2 shows the surface pore microstructure of a porous wick
with various pore-forming agent concentrations at a particle size of
d ¼ 2 lm. It is evident that large pores appear after the pore-forming
agent is cleared out, resulting in an increase in porosity and that the
large pores created by the pore-forming agent are meanwhile sur-
rounded by small pores created by nickel particles. As the wick is
placed into the LHP, small pores can exert a powerful capillary force,
and large pores, which are free at low pore-forming agent concentra-
tions [ Figs. 2(a) and 2(b)], provide a channel for vapor and
liquid. Several large pores can be seen connected to one another
as the amount of pore-forming agent concentration increases
[Fig. 2(c)].
Figure 3 shows the impact of the preparation method on the
wick’s microstructure of 2and 20 lm particles without pore-forming
agents (u ¼ 0%). It can be seen that the sintering neck of a CPS wick is
much larger than that of a LS one. For LS, nickel particles are in a point
contact state at the molding stage. However, for CPS, the pressure
applied to the powder body enhances the contact area of nickel par-
ticles or even causes a plastic deformation. After the sintering process,
the surface energy of the particles decreases, leading to a larger sinter-
ing neck and stronger connectivity between particles.

B. Porosity
Figure 4 shows the relationship between porosity, preparation
methods, pore-forming agent concentration, and particle size. It can
be seen that for a certain particle size and preparation method, with an
increase in pore-forming agent concentration, the porosity rises. After
the sintering and cleaning processes, the space occupied by the pow-
der’s pore-forming agent block would convert into large pores, as
shown in Fig. 2. The quantity of large pores grows along with the con-
centration of pore-forming agents, which raises porosity. Moreover,
the porosity of the loosely sintered wicks is much higher than that of
the CPS ones. Referring to Fig. 3, for the LS method, the arrangement
of nickel particles is disorganized. The contact type of the particles is a
point contact, which provides a large number of pores. However, the
CPS applies external force to the particles during the packaging
process, which rearranges the particles or even causes plastic deforma-
tion of the particles. The number of internal pores significantly
decreased as the particles’ type of interaction changed to surface con-
tact. The difference in porosity between these two approaches lessens
as the concentration of the pore-forming agent increases, because at
high pore-forming agent concentrations, large pores created by the
pore-forming agent instead of small pores formed by nickel particles
dominate the internal porous structure.
It is interesting that for LS, the porosity is independent of the
nickel particle size at u ¼ 0%, but increases as particle size increases
for CPS at u ¼ 0%. The reason behind this is that at u ¼ 0%, cold
pressing causes the particles to rearrange and compact, leading to the
formation of more pore spaces with larger particle powders, thereby
increasing porosity. In contrast, during the Loose Sintering process,

C. Particle connection strength
Most works of literature focus on the performance of the porous
wick, including its porosity, permeability, pore radius, and so on.
However, the connecting strength of the particles is also an important
factor that should be considered in applications. The off-powder rate
was used to represent the strength of the wick.
Figure 5 shows the off-powder rate under different condi-
tions. It is clear that when the concentration of the pore-forming
ingredient is less than 5%, the off-powder rate is almost negligible.
The off-powder rate rises sharply as the concentration of the pore-
forming agent rises. Because the pore-forming agent prevents
nickel particles from contacting one another and reduces the total
contact area, leading to a small sintering neck. As the pore-
forming agent concentration is high enough (over 20%), large
pores connect to each other and further reduce the connection
strength of nickel particles, as shown in Fig. 2 .M o r e o v e r ,t h er a t e
of off-powder rises as nickel particle size increases. The reason is
that under the same sintering conditions, larger particles have a
smaller sintering neck (Table). In addition, the off-powder rate of
CPS is much lower than that of LS, which also contributes to their
difference in sintering neck, as shown in Fig. 3 . The recommended
pore-forming agent concentrations for LS and CPS are 0% –10%
and 0%–20%, respectively, to ensure the working life of HP.
D. Capillary performance of the wick
A wick with excellent performance should have the following two
characteristics at the same time: (1) a powerful capillary force to propel
the working fluid ’s circulation; (2) a high permeability to lower the
working fluid’s flow resistance. However, the influence of porosity and
pore size distribution on these two characteristics is conflicting. The
intricate internal structure of the wick makes the influencing mecha-
nism complex. The capillary performance of the wick is the result of a
comprehensive balance of surface tension, contact angle, pore size,
porosity, and permeability, so the capillary performance can be utilized
to assess the wick’s overall performance.

IV. CONCLUSIONS
In phase-change heat dispersal systems like loop heat pipes, the
porous wick plays a crucial role. During the present study, 42 pieces of
nickel powder wicks were prepared by the sintering method. The
effects of powder size, pore-forming agent concentration, and prepara-
tion methods on the internal porous structure and capillary perfor-
mance of wicks were studied both experimentally and theoretically.
The following are the major conclusions:
(1) Although a wick composed of smaller particles is characterized
by a smaller average pore radius and a resultant stronger capil-
lary force, the presence of large sintering necks between the par-
ticles leads to low porosity. Hence, its performance is not as
good as that of sintered wicks with large particles. The addition
of the pore-forming agent effectively increases the porosity and
capillary performance of wicks while reducing strength.
Therefore, pore-forming agent concentrations for LS and CPS
are suggested to be 0% –10% and 0% –20%, respectively.
(2) The number, size, distribution, and connectivity of micro-pores
in the wick are influenced by particle size, pore-forming agent
concentration, and preparation methods, thus affecting the
properties of the wick. In the actual preparation process, the
synergistic effects of the above factors should be considered. For
example, high contact area and surface energy are exhibited by
small particles, which are conducive to the formation of sinter-
ing necks; thus, a lower sintering temperature and packaging
pressure are suggested compared to those for large particles in
order to obtain high porosity and permeability. Moreover,
much larger influence on the movement of large particles than
small particles is exerted by the addition of the pore-forming
agent, necessitating a higher sintering temperature or packaging
pressure for large particles to ensure good performance.
(3) A comprehensive model for capillary wick performance and
permeability has been successfully established by combining the
traditional Kozeny –Carman permeability formula with an
enhanced permeability equation specifically designed for pore-
forming agents. The enhanced permeability equation is opti-
mized for scenarios involving pore-forming agents. This model
demonstrates excellent predictive capabilities when handling
capillary wicks made from powders of small particle size and
those made from large particle size powders with low contents
of the pore-forming agent.
